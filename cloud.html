Cloud fundamentals

What Cloud First asks of you

The government introduced its Cloud First policy in 2013. The current wording of the Government Cloud First policy says that when procuring new or existing services, public sector organisations should default to public cloud first, using other solutions only where this is not possible. That approach is mandatory for central government and strongly recommended for the wider public sector. An organisation that does not deploy in public cloud should be able to evidence the decision, the business case and the value for money behind its choice.

Point 5 of the Technology Code of Practice is where the policy meets your plans. Its guidance, Use cloud first, says your plans must show you have considered cloud computing before other options, and that when you procure or renew services, all government organisations must consider and fully evaluate potential cloud solutions first.

The policy page adds one limit worth knowing early: solutions handling SECRET or TOP SECRET information are unlikely to be suitable for public cloud, and you should seek specialist advice. Neither page tells you which provider or service to choose. That belongs to the hosting strategy, and the Cloud strategy in a government context guide works through it: options, evidence, lock-in, data location and cost.

Buying cloud services

Crown Commercial Service became the Government Commercial Agency (GCA) on 1 April 2026, so framework guidance now sits on the GCA site. On cloud, G-Cloud 15 (RM1557.15) has replaced G-Cloud 14, and GCA lists its start date as 6 August 2026.

Before a design depends on a particular service, check with your commercial team that the service itself can be bought through the route they plan to use. The posts on what G-Cloud is and Digital Outcomes and Specialists 7 cover how the frameworks work and when you would use each.

UK regions and a second copy of your data

Each provider publishes where its regions are and how many zones each one has. These are the UK entries in the AWS Regions list, Microsoft's Azure region list and Google's regions and zones page.

UK cloud regions by provider
Provider Region and code Location Zones
AWSEurope (London), eu-west-2London3 Availability Zones
Microsoft AzureUK South, uksouthLondon3 availability zones
Microsoft AzureUK West, ukwestCardiffNone listed
Google CloudLondon, europe-west2London3 zones

AWS and Google Cloud each list one UK region, in London, with three zones. Azure lists two, UK South in London and UK West in Cardiff, and pairs them with each other. Microsoft lists availability zones for UK South but not for UK West.

That shapes disaster recovery. On AWS or Google Cloud, a second region means leaving the UK, for example AWS's Europe (Ireland) Region, eu-west-1. Whether that is acceptable is a data location decision. The Cloud guide for the public sector defines offshoring to include where data and services are physically located, who manages the services and who has access to the data. Whether you need a second region at all is a resilience question, which the resilience and disaster recovery guide covers from RTO and RPO onwards.

Before you design around a service, check it is offered in the region you need. Each provider keeps its own list: AWS Services by Region, Azure products by region and Google Cloud locations.
