One department needs a team to take a service through discovery. Another has developers but needs a specialist to untangle a difficult integration, and a council wants a partner to modernise several services while building the skills to look after them. Buying a discovery is a very different proposition from adding an architect to a team or appointing a partner for a whole programme, and Digital Outcomes and Specialists 7 covers all three, as long as you're clear which one you're buying.
 
# **What DOS7 is**
 
[Digital Outcomes and Specialists 7](https://www.gca.gov.uk/agreements/RM1043.9), usually shortened to DOS7, is a framework agreement with the reference RM1043.9. It's run by the Government Commercial Agency (GCA), which [took over from Crown Commercial Service](https://www.gca.gov.uk/about-gca/crown-commercial-service-ccs) on 1 April 2026, and it combines two earlier frameworks, Digital Outcomes 6 and Digital Specialists and Programmes.
 
Eligible public sector organisations can use it to buy agile digital, data and technology services across the whole lifecycle, from discovery through alpha, beta and live to retirement. The framework settles the supplier pool, contract terms and buying rules in advance, so each buyer runs its own competition and awards an individual contract under it, known as a call-off. That saves you building the commercial arrangement yourself, though you still have to define the requirement, evaluate suppliers and agree how delivery will be managed.
 
# **Four lots for four different needs**
 
DOS7 is split into four lots, described in the [framework specification](https://assets.gca.gov.uk/wp-content/uploads/RM1043.9-Framework-Schedule-1-Specification-v1.0-1.docx), and they can sound alike until you hold a real requirement up against them.
 
| Lot | What you buy | Example need | Maximum call-off |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Digital Outcomes | A supplier delivering a defined digital project using agile methods | A supplier to deliver a discovery | 48 months |
| 2 Digital Capability and Delivery Partners | Longer-term transformation, capability building and strategic resource augmentation | A partner to support several services over time and develop your internal capability | 72 months |
| 3 Digital Specialists | Individual specialists with particular digital skills for defined work | A solution architect to join your existing team for a defined piece of work | 24 months |
| 4 User Research Studios and Participants | Research space, and participants with the characteristics needed to test a service | Participants with particular accessibility needs | 36 months |
 
The maximum terms include any extensions and come from the [buyer guide](https://assets.gca.gov.uk/wp-content/uploads/RM1043.9-DOS-7-Buyer-Guide-v1.5.odt).
 
The lot sets the kind of support, but who carries responsibility for delivery is agreed separately for each statement of work, using the three accountability models the specification describes. Under Sole Responsibility the supplier carries the full risk of delivering the agreed milestones, and with Self-Directed Teams it produces increments the buyer commissions and carries some of the risk. With Rainbow Teams its staff work alongside yours and may be directed day to day by someone outside the supplier. The statement of work template says these align with the Outsource, Borrow and Bridge delivery models in the [Digital, Data and Technology Playbook](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-digital-data-and-technology-playbook).
 
The choice has tax consequences too, because the specification says it's likely to influence IR35 status, and it's the buyer's job to decide whether the work is a contracted-out service or a supply of resource. That's a conversation worth having before anyone writes a list of roles, since "we need six people" tells a supplier how much capacity you want and very little about what those people must achieve or what support they'll get from you.
 
Lot 4 is smaller but easy to underrate, because finding the right research participants takes real effort, and testing with colleagues who already know the process can miss problems a first-time user would hit straight away.
 
# **What changed with version 7**
 
DOS7 opened in January 2026 as an open framework under the Procurement Act 2023, which means it's a series of successive frameworks that let the supplier pool be refreshed. GCA says the arrangement will run for six years and reopen every 18 months. The current framework runs until 29 July 2027, and the second one, DOS 7.2 ([RM1043.9.2](https://www.gca.gov.uk/agreements/RM1043.9.2)), is due to open in summer 2027, with exact dates still to be confirmed.
 
GCA's [comparison with DOS6](https://assets.gca.gov.uk/wp-content/uploads/RM1043.9-key-changes-DOS6-to-DOS7-v1.0-1.odt) also lists revised award procedures and changes to the documents on intellectual property, security and sustainability. Even if you know DOS6 well, read them afresh, especially if an earlier procurement is your starting point.
 
# **How buying works**
 
Every DOS7 contract is awarded through further competition, with no option to award without one, and each procurement goes to a single supplier. You define your requirement and assessment criteria, invite eligible suppliers to respond, evaluate what comes back and complete the call-off contract, using a single-stage, two-stage or multi-stage selection process. The Contract Award Service, which you reach through a Public Procurement Gateway account, is the default place to publish requirements, and for Lot 3 you have to filter the supplier list first, so that only suppliers who can provide the role get invited.
 
A good specification tells suppliers what they're taking on, awkward parts included, such as a poorly documented legacy integration, an approval another team controls or restricted access to production data. Leave those out and the uncertainty just moves to after award. A discovery should still be free to change how the organisation understands its problem, but you can explain the questions you need answered and the evidence you expect at the end.
 
# **Where G-Cloud fits alongside it**
 
G-Cloud often comes up in the same conversation, and it has [its own post](https://thesolutionarchitect.uk/what-g-cloud-is-and-why-so-much-public-sector-technology-is-bought-through-it). [G-Cloud 15](https://www.gca.gov.uk/agreements/RM1557.15) covers cloud hosting, software and support, and its page sends anyone who needs bespoke design and development to DOS7.
 
Going the other way, DOS7 excludes standalone hosting, support and maintenance of a live application or website, and points buyers to G-Cloud 15 or, for major transformation programmes, Technology Services 4. So one service can mean several procurements, because the team that builds it doesn't necessarily come with the infrastructure it runs on or the support it needs afterwards, and those are worth planning together.
 
# **What matters for an architect**
 
Architects can do a lot to make the requirement clear before suppliers start writing. Start with the environment, meaning which systems the service has to integrate with, which platforms already exist, what data it'll handle, what recovery expectations apply, and which decisions the delivery team can make itself and which need agreeing elsewhere.
 
Then think about what the organisation needs to receive, because a demo might show that a feature works while whoever takes ownership also needs to understand the design, deployment, dependencies and how it behaves in operation. The specification requires knowledge transfer as part of handover and asks Lot 2 suppliers to build in-house capability, so it helps to say in the procurement what that should achieve. Could your own staff deploy a change, investigate a failure and explain the important design decisions once the supplier has gone?
 
Price needs the same care. The buyer guide explains the pricing mechanisms and how day rates can be checked against each supplier's framework maximums, but day rates won't tell you the whole cost of a service, and the guide itself says to weigh all costs over the life of the contract. Integration effort, internal staff time, operational support and the eventual exit all belong in that conversation.
 
A supplier's place on DOS7 only gets it into the competition, so you still have to judge the proposed team and approach against the work. Before the tender goes out, someone needs to work out who'll make decisions and how the supplier will work with your existing teams.
